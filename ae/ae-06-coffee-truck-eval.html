AE 06: Comparing Models with the Coffee Truck

Residuals, least squares, and R-squared

Important
  • Open RStudio and create a subfolder in your AE folder called “AE-06”
  • Go to the assignment for this activity in Canvas to get started.
  • Upload the ae-06-coffee-truck-eval.qmd file into the folder you just created. We will generate one data set for this activity shortly.

Intro

We’re back at Jo’s coffee truck. Today we’ll generate one data set, fit three models to it, and ask which one model is the “best” at predicting the number of Sales.

Important

This game’s data lands in a table that’s public to anyone, attached to your group name. Use the same team/Player Name from AE 05, or pick a new one that doesn’t identify you.

Recall the variables the game can record: Sales (our response), Location, Music, Price, and TimeOfDay.

Exercises

Generating and cleaning your data

Let’s put everything we’ve learned so far together into one big warm-up analysis. I have directions for you below, I recommend having someone in your group who isn’t the coder, open a link to the activity on the website so you don’t have to switch back and forth from the exercise block to the answer block. I want you to practice writing down a full analysis. Do this be interspursing your code with prose. Some tips:

  • Don’t write all of your code in one large chunk. This is difficult to follow.
  • Say what you’re going to do in prose, talking about the statistics rather than the code. For example say “Let’s make a boxplot to explore the relationship between…” rather than “Let’s apply gf_boxplot to…”. Try to explain what and why you’re going to do something rather than how you’re going to do it. The reader can see your code so they can see how you did it. Then write your code in a code chunk. After the output interpret the results. DO NOT just put output and leave that as your answer. Describe whaat you see. Remember that our goal is to predict Sales so describe what you analysis is saying about what is going to give you the most or least Sales.

On the ID screen, enter MATH2025FA26 as the Group Name and come up with a Team Name to enter as well (write it down and remember it).

  1. Set up your sim:
  • Price: select all four levels ($2, $3, $4, $5).
  • Time Of Day: select all three shifts (you may choose any three)
  • Hold everything else fixed: choose one (and only one) level for Location and one level for Music (it’s up to you)
  • Design Type: Random Sample and choose 15 repetitions.
  • Verify that it says 60 days and click Start Sim.
  • Click Get All Sales Data -> Continue -> Game Data
  • Enter the MATH2025FA26 for your group name and your Team Name from above in the PlayerID field.

  1. Download the CSV and upload it to your data folder.

  2. Load the packages we’ll use today, then read in your data with read_csv(). Confirm the number of rows is 60.

  3. Price was read in as a chr (character) column, not dbl (double), because the game exports it as text like "$3". Create a new column called PriceNum holding price as a number, using the same fix as before: strip the $ with str_replace(), then convert with as.numeric(). Leave the original Price column alone — we want it to stay categorical. glimpse() your data to confirm you now have both Price (categorical) and PriceNum (quantitative).

  4. Fit three models and display each with tidy(). The three models should all have Sales as the response variable and use the following as explanatory variables

    • fit_time: Explanatory = TimeOfDay, Response = Sales
    • fit_price_num: Explanatory = PriceNum, Response = Sales
    • fit_fitprice_cat: Explanatory = Price, Response = `Sales

  5. Last step: write all three estimated regression equations on the whiteboard. Whats the difference between the two Price models.

Evaluating your models

For each of your three models, use augment() to get fitted values, then compute \(R^2\) with rsq() and RMSE with rmse() (both from yardstick, with truth = Sales, estimate = .fitted).

Fill in the table below, and put it on the whiteboard next to your equations from Exercise 1.

Which model explains the most variation in Sales? Which would you recommend to Jo, and why?

All three models were fit to the same data set, so they all share the same SST. Why does that make this an apples-to-apples comparison, unlike comparing \(R^2\) across different data sets? What units is RMSE in here, and what does that number mean?

Compare fit_num and fit_cat, your two price models. Which has the larger \(R^2\)?

Why can the categorical version (fit_cat) never do worse than the quantitative one (fit_num) at explaining variation in Sales? What do you give up by using the categorical version anyway (think about parsimony, predicting at a price like $3.50, and having a single “per dollar” slope)?

(If time permits) Jo actually wants to maximize profit, not Sales — your data set also has a Profit column. Does the model with the highest \(R^2\) for Sales necessarily tell Jo the most about maximizing Profit? Explain.
